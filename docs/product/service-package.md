# Atelier-Kit commercial offer

This document defines the canonical commercial packaging for Atelier-Kit.

It describes **what GiadaWare sells and delivers**, what remains product
capability, what is optional service work, and where customer responsibility
begins.

Pricing is intentionally defined elsewhere. This document is the input contract
for pricing, onboarding, maintenance/upgrade and support.

Customer support obligations and boundaries are defined canonically in
[`customer-support-contract.md`](customer-support-contract.md).

## Commercial unit

The primary commercial unit is:

> **A configured, deployed and handed-off Atelier-Kit showcase, delivered as a bounded service around the open-source Atelier-Kit product.**

The customer is not buying a proprietary software licence.

They are buying a professional outcome:

- a working public showcase;
- initial configuration and content setup within an agreed scope;
- deployment and production verification;
- an authoring workflow appropriate to the engagement;
- handoff and enough guidance to manage ordinary content without becoming a
  webmaster.

Atelier-Kit itself remains open-source under the MIT licence.

## Target customer

The standard offer is intended for:

- artists;
- artisans and makers;
- authors and independent creative professionals;
- small studios;
- small creative activities that need a curated public presence.

The offer is strongest when the customer needs:

- a professional showcase rather than ecommerce;
- a bounded catalog or portfolio;
- occasional news/editorial content;
- direct contact actions;
- portable content and media under their own control;
- a simpler operating model than a generic CMS.

It is not intended for organizations that fundamentally require:

- ecommerce, cart or checkout;
- memberships or visitor accounts;
- public comments/community features;
- arbitrary page building;
- large editorial teams with complex roles;
- plugin ecosystems;
- multi-tenant SaaS behavior;
- a generic business application backend.

## Value proposition

Atelier-Kit packages a small but complete micro-CMS around one job:

> **Present a creative activity clearly online, with bounded authoring and
> publishing, without turning the customer into a site administrator.**

The differentiators are:

- curated instead of open-ended functionality;
- structured, human-readable YAML content;
- Git-backed editorial history;
- deployable, portable project ownership;
- bounded Studio authoring;
- no mandatory database;
- no proprietary content lock-in;
- explicit separation between public Visitor and authoring authority.

## Standard delivery

The standard commercial package includes the work needed to deliver one
customer-owned Atelier-Kit showcase using the product's supported capabilities.

### Discovery and setup

Included:

- one bounded intake/discovery step;
- creation or preparation of the customer project;
- initial Atelier-Kit configuration;
- choice/configuration of supported appearance options;
- configuration of site identity, contact points and public metadata;
- agreement on the initial content scope.

### Initial content population

The standard package includes an agreed bounded amount of initial customer
content using material supplied by the customer.

Typical initial content may include:

- About content;
- catalog/items;
- collections;
- news/editorial entries;
- contact information;
- supported images;
- Signal Clouds or public FAQ entries where useful.

The baseline standard package is sized for:

- up to **10 initial items**;
- up to **one collection**.

A commercial quote may explicitly vary those quantities without changing the
Atelier-Kit product contract.

The standard offer includes ordinary preparation of already-good material:

- typo and punctuation correction;
- small formatting or wording adjustments;
- normal image insertion;
- routine resizing, compression or supported format conversion.

It does not include substantial content creation, professional copywriting or
advanced image-production work unless separately quoted.

### Publication

Included:

- content/readiness validation;
- first production deployment;
- production smoke verification;
- connection of the agreed production URL;
- assistance with supported domain/DNS configuration where required.

### Authoring handoff

The customer receives an authoring path appropriate to the engagement.

The standard handoff may use:

- Local Studio;
- Atelier Desktop;
- another explicitly agreed supported authoring path.

The handoff includes a bounded walkthrough of:

- ordinary editing;
- preview;
- supported publishing workflow;
- basic operational boundaries.

## Hosted Studio

Private Hosted Studio is a **real Atelier-Kit capability**, but it is not
silently included in every standard customer delivery.

It is packaged as an optional managed/add-on capability when browser-accessible
private authoring is justified.

A Hosted Studio engagement may include:

- dedicated Hosted deployment;
- GitHub authentication/authorization setup;
- persistent session/security state;
- server-controlled repository authority;
- admitted Hosted authoring surfaces;
- Hosted-specific operational configuration and validation.

Hosted Studio does not turn Atelier-Kit into a generic multi-user SaaS CMS.

Its authority remains bounded by the Atelier-Kit Hosted architecture and by the
specific capabilities admitted for that deployment.

## Public Demo

The official Atelier-Kit Public Demo is a **commercial demonstration surface**.

Its purpose is to let prospective customers inspect the Visitor experience and
try a deliberately limited public authoring workflow.

It is:

- not a customer production environment;
- not a customer support environment;
- not private Hosted Studio;
- not evidence that every customer receives browser-hosted authoring by default;
- not a source of customer content or repository authority.

The Demo demonstrates product capability. Customer delivery is always a
separate project and deployment.

## Standard deliverables

A completed standard engagement normally delivers:

- one customer-specific Atelier-Kit project;
- the agreed initial content and configuration;
- customer-supplied images incorporated within scope;
- a working public production URL;
- supported domain configuration where agreed;
- the configured authoring workflow;
- production verification;
- basic handoff documentation/instructions;
- a **30-minute handoff session**;
- **one final correction/review pass** for supplied content mistakes or small
  configuration details within the agreed scope.

## Internal delivery baseline

For planning purposes, a normal standard first launch is expected to require
approximately **half a day to one day of operator work** when customer material
is substantially ready and the engagement stays within the baseline package.

This is an internal capacity assumption, not a customer-facing delivery-time
guarantee and not a price.

## Optional and bespoke work

Work outside the standard package may be quoted separately.

Typical optional work includes:

- private Hosted Studio setup;
- additional initial content population;
- substantial copywriting or editorial rewriting;
- professional photography or image production;
- large archive migration;
- translation;
- custom visual work beyond supported Atelier-Kit configuration;
- custom integrations;
- structural consumer-specific implementation;
- future Atelier-Kit upgrades;
- ongoing maintenance;
- publication assistance after handoff;
- managed support;
- custom development.

Optional work does not automatically become part of the core Atelier-Kit
product.

A customer-specific feature request may result in one of three outcomes:

1. configuration using existing product capability;
2. bespoke consumer work;
3. a separately evaluated upstream Atelier-Kit product change.

Those are intentionally different commercial and engineering decisions.

## Customer responsibilities

The customer is responsible for providing, as applicable:

- their public name/brand/project identity;
- substantially final source text;
- images and media they have the right to publish;
- factual product/item/project information;
- public contact details;
- an existing logo when one is to be used;
- timely review/approval;
- access or cooperation needed to configure customer-owned infrastructure.

The customer remains responsible for the legality and accuracy of their
published content.

## Customer ownership

Whenever reasonably possible, the customer owns and controls:

- their content;
- their images/media;
- their domain;
- their GitHub repository or agreed source repository;
- their Vercel project/account or agreed hosting account;
- other third-party accounts created for their project.

GiadaWare must not create artificial lock-in by retaining ownership of a
customer's domain or content infrastructure merely because it performed the
setup.

## GiadaWare responsibilities

Within the purchased scope, GiadaWare is responsible for:

- applying Atelier-Kit correctly;
- configuring supported product capabilities;
- performing the agreed initial setup/content integration;
- validating the project before handoff;
- carrying out the agreed first deployment;
- documenting material delivery assumptions;
- explaining the normal authoring/publishing workflow;
- respecting Atelier-Kit runtime and security boundaries;
- identifying out-of-scope work before performing it.

GiadaWare also owns upstream Atelier-Kit product evolution.

That does **not** imply that every future Atelier-Kit release, migration,
maintenance task or support request is included in the original customer
delivery.

Those obligations exist only when separately included or contracted.

## Delivery boundaries

The standard offer does not imply unlimited customization.

Unless explicitly quoted, it does not include:

- a new product feature;
- arbitrary visual redesign;
- generic CMS behavior;
- ecommerce;
- booking/payment systems;
- visitor accounts;
- community/comment systems;
- newsletter infrastructure;
- backend contact-form systems;
- multi-user editorial workflow;
- custom business applications;
- continuous maintenance;
- unlimited revisions;
- unlimited content entry;
- future upgrades;
- indefinite hosting administration;
- ongoing support.

Requests that materially change scope must be recognized before the extra work
starts and handled through a new quote, add-on or change of scope.

## Third-party services

Atelier-Kit deployments may depend on third-party services.

Typical dependencies include:

### GitHub

May provide:

- source repository hosting;
- Git-backed editorial history;
- repository authority for Hosted authoring.

Customer-specific repository ownership should remain with the customer whenever
practical.

### Vercel

May provide:

- public deployment;
- private Hosted Studio deployment;
- optional Web Analytics when explicitly enabled.

Free tiers may be sufficient for some projects, but Atelier-Kit does not
guarantee that a customer's use will remain within a third party's free plan.

### Domain registrar / DNS provider

A custom domain, when requested, is normally purchased and owned by the
customer.

### Redis / persistent security infrastructure

Private Hosted Studio or the Public Demo may require persistent server-side
state such as Redis.

This is not editorial storage and does not replace Git as the canonical
repository-backed content history.

## Third-party costs

Third-party fees are not implicitly included in the Atelier-Kit commercial
package.

Potential costs may include:

- domain registration/renewal;
- hosting plan charges;
- Vercel paid features;
- Redis or other infrastructure charges;
- other explicitly selected external services.

Where possible, those services should be billed directly to the customer
through customer-owned accounts.

Pricing and any GiadaWare handling/management fees are defined separately.

## Relationship between core, service and customization

The commercial model has three distinct layers.

### 1. Atelier-Kit core

The open-source product:

- runtime contracts;
- content model;
- Visitor rendering;
- Studio capabilities;
- publishing/upgrade tooling;
- supported integrations;
- security boundaries.

Core changes must remain product decisions, not accidental side effects of one
customer request.

### 2. Standard GiadaWare delivery

The repeatable service:

- intake;
- setup;
- configuration;
- bounded initial content population;
- deployment;
- verification;
- handoff.

This is the default commercial unit.

### 3. Customer-specific services

Separately scoped work:

- bespoke configuration/design;
- Hosted Studio deployment;
- migration;
- substantial editorial work;
- custom implementation;
- maintenance;
- upgrades;
- support.

Consumer-specific work must not silently redefine Atelier-Kit core.

## Success condition

A standard engagement is complete when:

- the agreed public showcase is online and verified;
- the agreed initial content is present;
- the chosen authoring path is configured;
- the customer knows how to perform ordinary supported updates;
- ownership boundaries are clear;
- third-party dependencies are known;
- any remaining optional work is explicitly outside the completed scope.

## Commercial principle

The customer pays for **delivery, expertise and an operational outcome**, not
for permission to use Atelier-Kit.

The intended outcome is:

> **A professional showcase under the customer's control, delivered through a
> repeatable GiadaWare service and maintainable without unnecessary technical
> dependency.**
